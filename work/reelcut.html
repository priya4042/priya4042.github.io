ReelCut: a video editor that never uploads your videos

How a tool that needed a PC became an Android app that edits everything on the phone - with no internet permission at all.

Platform
Android
Built with
Flutter, FFmpeg, ML Kit
Status
Ready for Google Play

The problem

Creators who film long videos - talks, classes, podcasts - want short vertical clips for Shorts and Reels. The popular tools upload the whole video to a server first, cost money, or both. I wanted something free that never sends anyone's video anywhere.

The first version, and why I threw it away

The first ReelCut was a phone remote for a program running on my PC: the phone sent the video, the PC cut it with FFmpeg and sent the clips back. It worked, but it meant a server, and a server means hosting costs and your videos on someone else's computer. Not what I promised.

What I built instead

  • All editing on the phone. The full FFmpeg video engine runs inside the app: cutting, cropping to 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5, titles, watermark and background music.
  • Face-aware cropping. Google's on-device ML Kit finds the face in a frame, so the vertical crop keeps the speaker in shot.
  • Highlights. It measures how loud each moment is and suggests the liveliest parts as clips.
  • No internet permission. The app literally cannot send data off the phone - and its Play Store data-safety form says "no data collected".

The hard part

A full video engine is big. Built for every kind of phone at once, the app was huge - so it ships as an Android App Bundle, and Google Play delivers only the version each phone's processor needs.

Where it is now

Finished, signed and ready for its Google Play listing. Free, with no ads and no account.

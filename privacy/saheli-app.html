Saheli privacy policy

Last updated: 2 November 2026

Saheli is made by Priya Tech Labs. It is a diary for periods and pregnancy, and what you write in it is personal health information. This policy explains, in plain words, what happens to it.

The short version

  • Everything you write in Saheli stays only on your phone.
  • There is no account, no sign-in and no server. We never see, collect, upload, sell or share your diary.
  • Android's automatic Google backup is turned off for this app, so your diary is not copied to your Google account or to a new phone by Android. The only copies are backup files that you make yourself.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. AdMob never receives anything you write in the diary.

What stays on your phone

Period start and end dates, flow, symptoms, mood, day notes, the temperature and weight you choose to write down, the temperature unit you picked, your usual cycle and period length, pill reminder names and times, and in pregnancy mode the last period date or due date, appointments, weight entries and notes. These are saved in the app's private storage on your phone. Other apps cannot read them. Uninstalling the app deletes them. You can also delete everything at any time from Settings, "Delete all data".

The estimates the app shows (next period, fertile days, likely ovulation, due date and pregnancy week) and the charts (cycle and period length, symptoms and mood by cycle day, temperature and weight) are worked out on your phone from what you entered. Nothing is sent anywhere to work them out. Temperature and weight are kept as plain numbers; the app does not work out ovulation or anything else from them.

Backups you make

If you choose "Save backup to Google Drive or a folder", Android's own save screen opens and the file goes only where you pick (for example your Google Drive, under Google's policy). If you choose "Share backup file", Android's share screen opens and the file goes to the app you choose. The app does not sign in to Drive and does not upload anything by itself. A backup file contains your whole diary, so keep it somewhere only you can open. Restoring works the same way: you pick the file and it is read on your phone. You then choose "Add to mine", which keeps the diary on the phone and adds the file's entries (when both have the same day, the one edited last is kept), or "Replace", which replaces the diary on the phone with the file. To make that choice possible, each entry carries the time it was last edited; this stays in the diary and in backup files you make.

Spreadsheet (CSV) export

If you choose "Save diary as a spreadsheet", the app first warns you that the file is not protected, then Android's own save screen opens and the CSV file goes only where you pick. It holds every logged day: dates, cycle day, flow, symptoms, mood, temperature, weight and notes. It is plain text that anyone who opens it can read, so keep it private. The app does not upload it anywhere.

Android's automatic Google backup is off

Many apps let Android copy their data into the user's Google account backup. Because Saheli holds health information, we have turned this off (`allowBackup="false"`, and the app's data extraction rules exclude everything from Google account backup and from phone-to-phone transfer). This means that if you change or reset your phone, your diary will not come back by itself. To keep it, save a backup file yourself before you change phones.

Summary for your doctor

When you share or print the cycle summary, the PDF or picture is made on your phone and handed to the app you choose through Android's share or print screen. What happens next is up to you and that app.

App lock

If you turn on app lock, the app asks Android to check your phone's own fingerprint, face, PIN or pattern. Android does the check and only tells the app whether it passed; the app never sees or stores your fingerprint, face or PIN. This uses the biometric permission.

Discreet mode and hiding the app

  • Reminder notifications are always set to hide their text on a secure lock screen.
  • In discreet mode, every notification only says "Reminder" and "Open the app to see it", and the home-screen widget shows only a number.
  • "Hide in recent apps" makes the app show blank in the recent apps list and blocks screenshots of the app.

Home-screen widget

If you add the Saheli widget, it shows the estimated days until your next period, or your pregnancy week in pregnancy mode. Anyone who can see your home screen can see it. Use discreet mode to show only a number, or remove the widget.

Reminders

If you turn on reminders, the app asks for permission to show notifications and schedules them on your phone: a few days before the estimated period, a daily diary reminder, the pill reminders you set and, in pregnancy mode, the day before each appointment. It re-schedules them after the phone restarts. The app only reminds you; it gives no medical or dose advice.

Advertising

The app shows ads from Google AdMob (a banner and an optional video that turns ads off for a day). AdMob may collect device information such as the advertising ID, approximate location from your IP address, and app interactions to show and measure ads. AdMob does not receive your diary, your dates, symptoms or anything else you write. See Google's policy: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

You can reset or turn off ad personalisation in your phone's Settings, under Google, Ads.

More apps

The "More apps from Priya Tech Labs" page opens our website in your browser. The links carry no tracking parameters.

New version notice

If you installed the app from our website, once a day it downloads a small version file from priya4042.github.io to tell you when a new version is ready. Nothing about you is sent: no account, no ID and no data from the app, just an ordinary web request to the site. This is not done when the app was installed from Google Play, which updates it for you.

Not medical advice

Saheli is a personal diary, not a medical device. It does not diagnose or treat anything, and its dates are estimates. Do not use it to prevent or plan pregnancy without a doctor.

Children

The app is meant for adults and is not directed at children under 13.

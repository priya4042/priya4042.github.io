Vehicle Book privacy policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

Vehicle Book is made by Priya Tech Labs. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app.

What stays on your phone

Your vehicles and their number plates, fuel fills, services, tyre and battery changes, expenses and challans, trips (including business trip purposes and your rate per km), vehicle loan details and EMIs, the papers, expiry dates and paper photos, warranties and bill photos, fuel station notes, and where you parked are stored only on your phone. A photo is taken with the camera or picked from the gallery only when you choose to add one. The app has no account and no server, and it does not connect to any government service. We never see, collect or upload your data.

Scanning a fuel bill

When you tap Scan bill, you take a photo of the bill or pick one from your gallery. The text is read on your phone by Google ML Kit text recognition, using a model that ships inside the app, so the photo and its text are not sent anywhere. Only the numbers you save are kept; the app does not keep the bill photo. Like all ML Kit features, it may send Google diagnostic and usage information such as the device model, OS version, app version, a per-installation identifier, how long recognition took and error codes, over an encrypted connection. See https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure

Backups are files that you create and save yourself, either by sending them to an app or by saving them through Android's own save screen to a place you pick, such as your Google Drive or a phone folder. The app does not sign in to Google Drive or use its API; Android hands the file over. From version 1.4.0 the backup file also contains your paper photos, and from 1.5.0 your bill and parking photos, parking spot, warranties and fuel station notes, so keep it somewhere safe. A travel claim PDF or a cost picture is made only when you tap Share, and goes only where you send it.

If Backup by Google is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may also copy the app's data into your own Google account backup, so it comes back when you set up a new phone. That backup belongs to your Google account; we never see it. You can turn it off in Settings, Google, Backup.

Parking location

When you note where you parked, you can tap Add my location. Only then does the app ask for location permission, and only while the app is open; it never reads your location in the background or on its own. It reads one GPS point, keeps it on your phone with your parking note, and removes it when you tap I've left or remove it. When you tap Open in Maps, the point is handed to the map app you choose (or opened in Google Maps in your browser), which then follows its own privacy policy. You can say no to the permission; the note and photo still work.

App lock

App lock is off until you turn it on in Settings. It uses your phone's own fingerprint, face, PIN, pattern or password through Android. The app never sees or stores your fingerprint, face or screen lock; Android only tells it whether the check passed.

Notifications

When you add a paper or a loan, the app asks for permission to show notifications and schedules reminders on your phone: before a paper expires, a day before each EMI, before a warranty ends, before paid parking ends when you set a parking reminder, when a service is near by date or by the kilometres on the meter, and a gentle note when tyres or the battery pass the limits you set. It also re-schedules them after the phone restarts. You can turn reminders off in Settings at any time.

Advertising

The app shows ads from Google AdMob. AdMob may collect device information such as the advertising ID, approximate location from your IP address, and app interactions to show and measure ads. See Google's policy: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

You can reset or turn off ad personalisation in your phone's Settings, under Google, Ads.

Children

The app is meant for adults and is not directed at children under 13.

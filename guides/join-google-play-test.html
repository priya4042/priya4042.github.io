How to join an Android app's test on Google Play

20 October 20263 min readPriya Kumari

What a closed test is, why new apps need 12 testers for 14 days, and the four steps to join one.

Before a new app from a new developer account can go public on Google Play, Google asks for a closed test: a small group of real people who install the app and keep it for 14 days in a row. For new personal developer accounts the group must be at least 12 testers. It is how Google checks that an app works on real phones before the whole world can download it.

Testing costs nothing, takes a minute to set up, and you get the app before everyone else.

The four steps

  1. Send your Gmail address. The developer adds it to the test list. It has to be the Google account you use for the Play Store on your phone.
  2. Open the invite link. You'll get a link that says Become a tester. Tap it while signed in to the same Google account and accept.
  3. Install from the Play Store. The invite page has a Download it on Google Play link. The app installs like any other app and updates itself.
  4. Keep it installed for 14 days. That's the part that counts. Open it now and then, and if something feels wrong, say so - feedback during the test is exactly what it's for.

Questions testers ask

Will the developer see my data? They see the email address you sent them. The app itself works the same as the public version will.

Can I leave the test? Yes, from the same invite link, any time. Please stay for the 14 days if you can - leaving early can reset the count for the developer.

Do I need to pay? No.

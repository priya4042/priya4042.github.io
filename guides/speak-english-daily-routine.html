Speak English confidently: a 15-minute daily routine

20 October 20264 min readPriya Kumari

Most people who understand English but freeze when speaking need practice, not more grammar. Fifteen minutes a day, five parts.

If you can read and understand English but your mind goes blank when you have to speak, the problem usually isn't grammar. It's that speaking is a physical skill, and skills need daily practice. This routine takes fifteen minutes and can be done anywhere you can talk out loud.

Minutes 1-3: warm up your mouth

Say five difficult words slowly and clearly, then at normal speed - words you often mispronounce, like development, comfortable or vegetable. Listen to the correct pronunciation first and copy it exactly.

Minutes 4-6: shadowing

Play a short clip of clear spoken English - a news reader, a teacher, a dialogue - and speak along with it, a fraction of a second behind. Copy the rhythm and where the voice goes up and down, not just the words. This is the fastest way to start sounding natural.

Minutes 7-10: one useful phrase, three ways

Pick one everyday phrase - "Could you help me with...", "I was wondering if...", "What I mean is..." - and make three sentences with it about your own life. Say them out loud. Phrases you have actually said come back to you when you need them.

Minutes 11-14: talk about your day

Describe your day out loud for three minutes: what you did, what went wrong, what you're planning. Record it on your phone. Don't stop to correct yourself - the goal is to keep talking.

Minute 15: listen back to one thing

Play the recording and notice just one thing to improve tomorrow - a word you paused on, a sentence you couldn't finish. One thing a day adds up quickly.

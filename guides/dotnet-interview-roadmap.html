.NET interview prep: what to study, in what order

20 October 20264 min readPriya Kumari

A roadmap for .NET developers preparing for product-company interviews - from C# fundamentals to system design and stories.

.NET interviews at product companies usually test four things: the language and runtime, data and APIs, problem solving, and how you talk about your own work. Studying them in the right order saves weeks.

1. C# and the runtime (week 1-2)

  • Value vs reference types, boxing, structs vs classes, immutability and records
  • Generics, delegates, events, lambdas and LINQ (and when LINQ is slow)
  • async/await: what a Task is, why async void is dangerous, deadlocks and ConfigureAwait
  • Garbage collection basics, IDisposable and the using statement

2. ASP.NET Core, data and SQL (week 2-3)

  • The middleware pipeline, dependency injection and service lifetimes
  • REST design, status codes, authentication vs authorisation
  • Entity Framework Core: tracking, N+1 queries, migrations
  • SQL: joins, indexes, transactions and isolation levels - expect to write a query on the spot

3. Data structures and algorithms (week 3-6)

Don't solve random problems. Learn the patterns - two pointers, sliding window, binary search, BFS/DFS, heaps, dynamic programming - and practise a few problems for each. A well-known list like the Blind 75 covers most of what comes up.

4. System design (week 5-7)

For mid-level roles and above: caching, queues, databases vs search indexes, scaling reads and writes, and how you'd design something familiar, like a URL shortener or a notification service. Practise explaining the trade-offs out loud.

5. Your stories (throughout)

Prepare five or six real stories from your work in the STAR format - situation, task, action, result: a hard bug, a disagreement, a deadline, something you'd do differently. Many companies, Amazon among them, weigh these as heavily as the coding rounds.

Practise offline

DotNet Interview Prep follows this order: about 1,300 questions with answers across C#, .NET, SQL, DSA and system design, the Blind 75 and 18 DSA patterns, mock interviews, spaced revision and a STAR story builder. Everything works offline.

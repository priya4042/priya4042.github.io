How to run a committee (BC) fairly: a simple checklist

20 October 20264 min readPriya Kumari

Fix the rules before the first collection, write everything down, and keep everyone informed - a checklist for small savings groups.

A committee - also called a BC, kitty or chit - is a group that saves together: everyone puts in the same amount every cycle, and each cycle one member takes the whole pot. Most fights in a committee don't come from money going missing. They come from rules nobody wrote down. This checklist is for small groups of friends, family or colleagues.

Large or commercial chit funds are regulated by law in India. This guide is only about informal groups.

Before the first collection

  • Amount and members. How much per person, how many people, and so how big the pot is.
  • Cycle and date. Monthly or weekly, and the exact day money is due.
  • Who gets the pot when. A fixed turn order, a lucky draw each cycle, or bidding - where a member who needs money early accepts a little less, and the difference is shared by the others. Choose one and stick to it.
  • Late payments. How many days of grace, and whether there is a late fine.
  • What if someone leaves. Agree it now, while everyone is friendly.

During the committee

  • Record every payment the day it arrives - who, how much, how (cash or UPI).
  • Announce each payout to the whole group, with the amount.
  • Send reminders before the due date, not after.
  • Share a statement every cycle: collected, paid out, cash in hand.

Why I made an app for it

A notebook works until someone asks "did I pay in March?". Committee Book keeps the records, sends WhatsApp and UPI reminders, handles turn order, lucky draw and bidding, and makes PDF statements. Members can see their own status once the organiser approves them.
